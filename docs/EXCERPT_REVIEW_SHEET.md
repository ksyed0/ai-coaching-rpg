# Excerpt probe review sheet (AC-0191)

Seven real, redacted session excerpts from the Friday Escalation set. The levels the controlling agent assigned are deliberately NOT shown, so you rate blind.

## How to use this sheet

1. For each probe, read the transcript and look only at what the **subject** says (their lines are marked with `>>`). Other people's lines are context, not evidence.
2. Pick the level whose anchor best matches the subject's own words. If between two anchors, choose the lower only when the higher anchor's key behaviour is clearly missing.
3. Write your level (1 to 4, or `NO` for not observed / no opportunity) on the `Your level:` line, plus an optional note.
4. Send the filled sheet back (or tell me the seven levels) and I will compare them with the assigned levels, update the probe files where you differ, tick AC-0191 and re-run the probe checks.

Scale: 1 Not yet demonstrated, 2 Developing, 3 Proficient, 4 Advanced. Level 1 means there was a clear opportunity and the behaviour is absent from the subject's own lines.

## 1. `exc-listening-01`

Criterion: **Listening**. Subject: **account_manager**.

What it measures: Takes in what others say, in the team and with the client, and builds on it rather than talking past it. The key behaviour is visibly responding to another person's words. (Asking the client about the need is Discovery, not Listening.)

Anchors:

- Level 1: There was a question or point addressed to the participant and their own next line does not respond to it: it repeats a position already stated, or changes the subject.
- Level 2: Acknowledges what was said ("I hear you", "fair point") but the next statement does not use it or show what was understood.
- Level 3: At least two of the following: paraphrases or summarises another person's point accurately; checks that they understood it; makes the next statement visibly build on what was said.
- Level 4: Uses another person's own words, concern or figure to connect to the team's position or to move the conversation forward, and brings an overlooked or quieter point back in.

## 3. `exc-negotiation-01`

Criterion: **Negotiation**. Subject: **account_manager**.

What it measures: Protects scope, margin and the relationship together by offering options with their trade-offs and by attaching conditions to concessions, rather than conceding or refusing outright. The key behaviour is trade-offs and conditional concessions. (Naming prices, terms and commercial mechanisms is Commercial judgement, scored separately.)

Anchors:

- Level 1: There was an opportunity to propose a way forward and the participant's own lines either concede the request outright or refuse it outright, with no alternative and no condition.
- Level 2: Offers one alternative without saying what it costs or risks, or without a condition attached.
- Level 3: At least two of the following: offers more than one option; states what each option costs or risks (scope, date, risk to launch); ties a concession to something in return, such as a change request, a sign-off or an agreed date.
- Level 4: Recommends one option with reasons, holds its conditions when challenged, and checks that the other side accepts the terms by stating the agreed next steps back (who sends what by when).

Transcript:

>> [s1_huddle] **account_manager**: I'm worried that a flat refusal will damage the relationship, especially since the renewal is coming up. Let's see if there's a middle ground or a phased approach that doesn't compromise the go-live date.

   [s1_huddle] **delivery_lead**: I agree we can't risk the date, but we can't just say no. What if we offer this as a phase two delivery, properly scoped and priced, to happen immediately after go-live?

   [s1_huddle] **tech_lead**: I can live with that. A phased version would be roughly half the effort and keeps the go-live date safe.

>> [s1_huddle] **account_manager**: Let's go with that. We'll position it as a phase two delivery and price it as a change request, but we need to be very clear with her about the risk to the date if we tried to squeeze it in now.

## 5. `exc-role_clarity-02`

Criterion: **Role clarity**. Subject: **tech_lead**.

What it measures: An individual behaviour: what THIS participant says about who owns what, and whether they stay within their own area and hand over the rest. (How well the team as a whole used its members' roles is scored in the group rubric as role_clarity_group.)

Anchors:

- Level 1: There was a question or a follow-up that needed an owner and the participant's own lines name none, or the participant answers a question that belongs to a teammate (a technical or commercial one) without handing it over.
- Level 2: Takes ownership of their own area when asked, but does not name owners for the other items.
- Level 3: At least two of the following: names clear owners for key items (presenter, timeline, price); hands a technical or commercial question to the teammate who owns it; confirms the owners at the end.
- Level 4: Assigns and confirms ownership with names and dates, invites the owner to speak at the right moment, and puts ownership right when it blurs.

## 6. `exc-team_alignment-01`

Criterion: **Team alignment**. Subject: **delivery_lead**.

What it measures: An individual behaviour: what THIS participant does to bring the team to one position before engaging the client, and to keep to it in front of the client. (Whether the team as a whole ended up with the same picture of the facts is scored in the group rubric as shared_understanding.)

Anchors:

- Level 1: There was a team huddle or a decision point and the participant's own lines push a personal position without asking others, or contradict a teammate in front of the client.
- Level 2: Shares a view and invites others' views, but does not propose one position or check that the team agrees to it.
- Level 3: At least two of the following: asks the others for their views; proposes one position; checks that each teammate agrees to it before the client is engaged; restates the agreed position.
- Level 4: Resolves a difference actively (asks the sceptic what would make it acceptable), restates the agreed position and its fallbacks, and supports teammates when they speak in front of the client.
